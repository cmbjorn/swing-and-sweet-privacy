Every track at the right level, all night.

Swing & Sweet is a sound leveler for DJs who play vintage jazz and blues for dancing. It evens out the level between tracks and inside them, tames loud brass and hard drums, cleans up worn 78s and keeps the peaks safe for the PA, from Spotify, Music, YouTube, DJ software or its own player.

What it does

A sound engineer for the dance floor

A 1928 transfer and a 2019 remaster can sit 10 dB apart and sound like they come from different planets, and a single track can go from a whispered piano solo to a shout chorus. Ordinary volume normalization gives each track one setting and leaves the track itself alone. Swing & Sweet listens 2.5 seconds ahead of what the dancers hear and works on the music as it plays, between the tracks and inside each one, so you can set the mixer once and pay attention to the floor.

Leveling, inside every track

Every track at the same loudness, and inside it a quiet solo or a soft vocal chorus is lifted while a loud shout chorus is held back. Breaks and stop-time are never lifted, and once the app knows a track, a quiet solo is lifted from its first note.

Loud brass and hard drums

A screaming trumpet section, a crashing cymbal or a snare that cracks through the room is softened only while it is hard on the ears. Bass is kept steady from track to track.

Restore

Removes clicks and crackle and lowers hiss on worn 78 transfers. In Auto it decides per track, early, and never switches off in the middle of one.

Room and Ambience

Room fits the sound to the venue, from Padded 3 for a damped room full of people, through Neutral, to Boomy 3 for a reverberant hall. Ambience adds a touch of space to very dry recordings.

Safe peaks

A true-peak limiter protects the PA. Limiter only gives one track just the level match and the limiter, for the rare recording where you want it untouched.

It learns your tracks

Each play leaves a few numbers about the track: its level, breaks and solos. Next time the Known light is on and the track is right from the first second.

Fit the room, and set the volume of each track once

Room fits the sound to the venue, from Padded 3 for a damped room full of people, through Neutral, to Boomy 3 for a reverberant hall. Set it once when you arrive.

Vol-mem. sets the volume of one track, up to 6 dB either way, and the next time that track plays it starts there. A weak transfer stays lifted and a ballad stays lower, without touching anything. It is separate from the master Volume, which stays your fader for the whole night.

Getting started

Up and running in a minute

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 14.2 or later.
  • Music: Spotify, Music, YouTube in a browser, another player or DJ program, or your own files in the built-in player.
  • At a dance: a cable from the Mac's headphone jack to a line input on the mixer (not a microphone input).

Four steps

  1. Connect the PA. Headphone jack to a line input on the mixer.
  2. Start your music app and play a track. Set its own volume to 100 % and its crossfade to 0 s.
  3. Press Power. The first time, macOS asks for System Audio Recording (so Swing & Sweet can take the music app's sound; nothing is recorded) and, for Spotify and Music, Automation (so it notices pause, skip and seek). Allow both.
  4. Set the mixer gain once, by ear. Every track now comes out at the same loudness, so that one setting holds for the evening.

The first Power on offers the free 14-day trial. Pressed with music playing, the music goes on after a very short break and the processed sound fades in over the next minute; pressed in a pause, the first track starts processed.

Sources

Play from the app you already use

Swing & Sweet takes the sound of one app directly, with no extra drivers, and silences that app's own output while it runs, so only the processed sound is heard. Choose the app in Setup → Source; any app that is making sound shows up there within a couple of seconds.

Spotify and Apple Music

Fully followed

Pause, skip and seek are followed at once, and the app remembers each track: its notes, its Vol-mem. and Limiter only.

  • Spotify: in the devices menu choose This computer, crossfade 0 s, Automix off, volume 100 %.
  • Music: play to this Mac, not to an AirPlay speaker.

YouTube and other sites

From the sound

Choose the browser in Setup (Google Chrome, Firefox, Safari and so on). Track changes are found from the silences between tracks, so a pause is heard as a silence and no track notes are kept.

  • Every tab of the browser is processed: close the ones you do not need.
  • Adverts and autoplay gaps count as silences, and are levelled like music.

Other players

From the sound

A file player, TIDAL, VLC or any other app is listed while it plays. Music that begins after a silence of about 4.5 s starts processed within half a second; between two tracks that follow each other it comes about 2.5 s after you press play.

DJ software

Try it at home first

djay, Traktor, Serato, Virtual DJ, Mixxx and similar programs are listed like any other app, but they have not been tested yet. Swing & Sweet works from their sound; a fade-out with a fader is followed and never lifted by more than about 2 dB.

  • If the program sends a headphone cue to the same Mac, the cue may be silenced or mixed into the room: check before a dance.

While it runs, Swing & Sweet moves the Mac's alert sounds to the built-in speakers and moves them back when it stops. Other apps' own sounds (a call, a video) are not touched: turn on Focus (Do Not Disturb) for the evening.

Learning

Learn while playing is on by default and changes nothing you hear. Delete all notes… starts again from nothing. Notes are numbers only, never sound.

The built-in player

Run the whole evening from one window

For a set from your own music files, without Spotify or a DJ program. Press the list icon on the panel or ⌥⌘L, and the panel turns into the player in the same window. Press Play and the processing turns itself on: you never press Power here. Because the player knows its files, every track starts at once and the gap between tracks is exact.

Amber is the room

The track at the top, Up Next, Play and Stop: what the dancers hear. No key starts music in the room; only the Play button does.

Blue is only your headphones

The pre-listen bar at the bottom. Nothing blue ever reaches the room.

In the room. The track playing for the dancers, its tempo and a TAP key. The time left is large, so you see it from across the booth.

Stop, Next now and End after this track. Next now cuts the track and starts the next one. End after this track lets the room go quiet when the track ends, for an announcement, and switches itself off.

Seek in the room. A click on the bar under the time plays the track from there; the small keys play it from the start, 10 s back or 10 s on. The processing catches up as after a seek in Spotify.

Its shape. Once a track is analysed, its bar shows how loud it is through the music: a quiet intro, a break or a loud last chorus, to click straight to.

Your library

Add Music Folder… reads a folder and its subfolders: MP3, AAC, Apple Lossless, FLAC, WAV and AIFF. The files stay where they are and are never changed.

  • Search every word in title, artist, album or tag.
  • Filter by a BPM range, by tags, and Hide played tonight. Click a tag on a track to filter by it; + adds a tag to every selected track.
  • Sort by any column: year, length, BPM, artist.
  • A track already in Up Next shows its place there in amber after its title.

The Sound column

In the background Swing & Sweet listens to your whole library, one file at a time, through the same processing the room uses (about four seconds per track on an M1). Then, before a track has ever played, you can see:

  • The lift: +10 is a quiet old record raised 10 dB, −3 a loud modern one turned down.
  • The grade: one red bar is poor, two orange rough, three yellow fair, four green good.
  • An amber R when Restore in Auto will take the track. Click it for the details in words.

The analysis also gives the room its note of the track, so it plays at the right level from the first second of its first play. Pause the analysis during a dance on a small Mac, and let it run overnight.

Up Next

Drag tracks to Up Next, or select them and press Return. The first three are what comes next; the rest wait under Later. Drag to reorder; Delete or the ✕ takes one out.

  • Planning the set. The top says how many tracks, how long, and when the queue will have played (ends 18:13), with a small curve of the tempo through the set.
  • Gentle marks. A track gets a small mark when it has the same artist as the one before, or a tempo more than about a third away from it. Nothing is stopped; it is only for you to see.
  • Pause between tracks is the silence between two tracks, 2 s to start with. An optional crossfade (2 to 8 s) is under Engineer, off by default.
  • Playlists. Save as Playlist… keeps tonight's list. Playlists are written as M3U8 for other players and read in from them (Import Playlist…).

Pre-listen. Double-click a track, or select it and press Space, to hear it in your headphones. Click the bar to jump into the middle; the small player has previous, back 10 s, play/pause, stop, forward 10 s and next, and [ ] step through the list as it is shown.

At the room's level. The headphones are not processed, but once a track is analysed they get the lift the room will give it, so a quiet old record sounds about as loud in your ears as on the floor. A limiter keeps a lifted track from clipping in your ears.

Headphones of their own. Pre-listen needs a device of its own, such as Bluetooth headphones or a USB headphone adapter: the Mac's jack goes to the room. Choose them by name; their level has its own − and +.

Tempo, the way swing DJs count. BPM in quarter notes per minute, measured or taken from the tag; an unsure value has a ?. TAP in time (or press T) to fix it, ×2 and ÷2 for a tempo counted in halves, Right to confirm. Every tempo you confirm teaches the analysis what swing sounds like in your music.

The sound card

The panel's controls, standing up along the right edge: Leveling, Room, Ambience, Restore, Limiter only, the meters In and Out with their lights, and Vol-mem. for this track. Volume is at the bottom, with ⌘↑ and ⌘↓.

Engineer (⇧⌘E) unfolds the engineer panel under it. The card has no Power key: Play turns the processing on, and the light by the name shows it.

If something goes wrong

The player has no unprocessed fallback: if Swing & Sweet stops, the room goes quiet. When it opens again it offers to go on from there, with the same Up Next and the track where it was.

Names from the files. When tracks have no artist tag but their file names say it (1924 Ma Rainey - Lucky Rock Blues), the player offers to use the names. The files are never changed.

Before the first dance

A checklist for the evening

  • System Settings → Sound: keep Output on Headphones (the jack). Your music app then plays on there if anything fails.
  • Leave Keep notification sounds off the output on in Setup.
  • Turn on Focus (Do Not Disturb) and quit other apps that make sound.
  • Spotify: crossfade 0 s, Automix off, highest quality, volume 100 %.
  • With a mixer, Volume at 0 and the level on the mixer's fader. Without one, set the speakers once and use Volume as your fader.
  • Keep a reserve source on its own mixer channel, such as a phone with a playlist.

If something goes wrong, the music does not stop. If Swing & Sweet quits or hangs (a watchdog ends a hang within half a second) or you press Power off, your music app plays on, unprocessed, on the jack. The built-in player is the exception: it is part of the app.

Questions

Common questions

The status line says “Could not start”

Usually the System Audio Recording permission is missing. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → System Audio Recording (in some macOS versions: Screen & System Audio Recording), allow Swing & Sweet and press Power again.

Pause and skip in Spotify or Music are not followed

Allow Automation: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation → Swing & Sweet, and turn on Spotify or Music. Browsers and other apps have no such link; there Swing & Sweet works from the sound and hears a pause as a silence.

Spotify plays, but nothing reaches Swing & Sweet

Spotify is sending the music straight to a speaker (Spotify Connect), past the Mac. In Spotify's devices menu (the speaker icon at the bottom), choose This computer. The status line says so after 5 seconds.

My app is missing from the source list

Start its music: it appears within a couple of seconds, and stays in the list for 10 minutes after it stops, so you can pause it and then choose it. For YouTube, choose the browser. Background services never appear.

The sound stutters or drops out

The Mac could not keep up; the status line then says Too much load. Quit other heavy apps and press Power again. On a small Mac, pause the player's library analysis during the dance. If it happens again, write to me with the time and what else was running.

A track sounds wrong after processing

Press Limiter only: that track then gets only the level match and the limiter, and it is remembered for its next play. Please write and tell me the track, the source and what you heard, so I can make it better.

A notification sound reached the sound system

Swing & Sweet moves the Mac's alert sounds to the built-in speakers, but other apps can still play sounds of their own: a call, a calendar alarm, a video. Turn on Focus (Do Not Disturb) before the dance and allow no people and no apps.

Pre-listen has no headphones to choose

Pre-listen needs headphones on a device of their own, such as Bluetooth headphones or a USB headphone adapter. The Mac's own jack goes to the room, so it is never used for pre-listen, and neither is AirPlay.

Can I play to AirPlay, Sonos or Bluetooth speakers?

Yes, mostly at home. In Spotify choose This computer, in Control Center → Sound pick the speaker, and in Setup → Output choose Same as the system output. Turn Volume down before you quit: without Swing & Sweet the music plays a little louder.

Does Swing & Sweet record or upload my music?

No. It holds a few seconds of sound in memory to process it, and never stores audio. It makes no network connections of its own and has no analytics or tracking. The numbers it keeps per track stay on your Mac. See the privacy policy.
